Adaptive Productivity · AI · Product Management

Navira — Adaptive Life Companion

Can productivity tools reduce decision fatigue by adapting to a user's energy, mood, workload, and real-life circumstances?

Stage: User Research
View on Notion →
What I'm building
  • Adaptive daily planning based on energy, mood, stress, deadlines, and available time
  • Intelligent task prioritization and automatic rescheduling when plans change
  • AI companion with Listener, Planner, and Coach modes
  • Stress recovery with guided breathing, meditation, and optional reflection
  • Student workspace for tasks, notes, documents, assignments, and study planning
  • Optional menstrual-cycle-aware planning to help users align their workload with changing energy levels
  • Web + mobile experience with a shared backend
What I explored
  • User research to understand why students and professionals struggle to act on tasks they genuinely want to complete
  • Decision fatigue and cognitive overload as an alternative explanation to "lack of motivation"
  • Adaptive scheduling rather than rigid task management
  • Designing AI that listens before giving advice
  • Human-centered productivity experiences that prioritize sustainable progress over streaks and completion counts
  • Product discovery, MVP prioritization, UX design, and end-to-end product development
  • Building a cross-platform product from concept through validation and launch
Product Insight

Most productivity apps help users remember what to do. Navira is designed to help them decide what to do next.

Current Outcome

Navira is being developed as an end-to-end product case study, beginning with problem validation and user research and progressing toward a functional web and mobile MVP.

AI-Powered Interview Coach with Evaluated Feedback Loops

Can an LLM agent give interview feedback that's consistently rigorous — not just plausible-sounding — across hundreds of different questions and JDs?

PythonFastAPINext.js Claude APIpgvectorLangGraph
View on GitHub →
What I explored
  • RAG over a curated question bank + the specific job description to generate role-relevant interview questions rather than generic ones
  • Structured function-calling to force consistent, scoreable feedback (not free-text opinions) on each answer
  • A hand-rolled agent state machine to manage the multi-turn interview flow (ask → answer → follow-up → score)
  • An eval harness — golden Q&A pairs with expected feedback quality — to measure whether prompt changes actually improved output, rather than trusting vibes
Measured outcomes

Built and ran a 40-case eval suite across three prompt iterations; the third version cut inconsistent scoring (same-quality answers rated differently) and reduced vague feedback (flagged by rubric, not by eye) — gains that came from tightening the scoring schema, not from a bigger model.
